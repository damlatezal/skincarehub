What are the Types of Chemical Acids?

Published on Feb 12, 2023

Correct and effective use of chemical acids

In order to benefit most effectively from chemical acids in skin care, it is necessary to first recognize the types of acids, how they work on the skin and which skin problems they are used to target. Different types of chemical acids are specially formulated to solve specific skin problems. Therefore, understanding the properties and areas of use of each acid can help you optimize your skin care routine. We have examined the types of chemical acids used in skin care in detail and determined in which cases these acids are most effective.

1. AHA (Alpha Hydroxy Acids)

These acids are a family of fruit-derived carboxylic acids. Examples of AHAs: mandelic acid from bitter almonds, glycolic acid from sugar cane, lactic acid from milk, citric acid from citrus fruits, etc..

2. BHA (Beta Hydroxy Acids)

In cosmetics, the term beta hydroxy acid refers specifically to salicylic acid. Salicylic acid works primarily as an exfoliant. Salicylic acid is also used in acne treatments to combat inflammation. It works in the lower layers of the skin, not on the surface like AHAs, and can reach the dermis by penetrating into the pore. Therefore, it provides more effective pore cleaning and is effective in problems such as blackheads and large pores.

3. PHA (Polyhydroxy Acids)

Polyhydroxy acids are ingredients such as gluconolactone and lactobionic acid. They have similar effects to AHAs, but since they have a larger molecular structure, they have a lower risk of causing sensitivity on the skin. They provide a gentler exfoliation and are good for roughness and blemishes on the skin surface. They are chemical acids that are also suitable for sensitive skin. They also help improve the skin surface by acting as antioxidants..

4. Azelaic Acid

Azelaic acid is a naturally occurring acid found in grains such as barley, wheat, and rye. It has antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties that make it effective in treating skin conditions such as acne and rosacea. This acid clears acne-causing bacteria from pores and prevents new acne from forming. Azelaic acid is a gentler exfoliant than glycolic and salicylic acids, making it more suitable for sensitive skin.
